The honest truth

Why waiting, drugs and massage have not given you real progress

You have probably already spent a lot on:

  • Just wait and see. Some nerves do need time. But waiting with no exercise can leave joints stiff and muscles weak. Guided movement while the nerve recovers makes a real difference.

  • Massage and traditional treatment. Rubbing a weak limb may feel comforting, but it does not retrain the muscles. Pulling on a limb with a nerve injury can damage the shoulder or other joints.

  • Drugs and vitamins. They may help with pain, but they do not move your joints or retrain your muscles.

  • Waiting for surgery or a specialist. Some serious nerve injuries need a specialist or surgery, and your doctor will guide you. Even then, rehabilitation before and after matters.

  • Not using the weak limb. Leaving an arm or foot unused can lead to stiffness, thinning of the muscles and even weaker movement.

  • Random exercises from YouTube. These can strain a weak, unprotected shoulder or foot. Nerve injuries need carefully chosen, gentle exercises.

What is really happening

Nerve injuries do not get better with drugs or massage alone, because the real problems are in the nerve, the muscles it controls and the joints around them:

  • An injured nerve. In Erb's palsy, the nerves running from the neck to the shoulder and arm were stretched or damaged, often at birth but sometimes after an accident. In injection palsy, a nerve was injured near an injection site, which can weaken the arm, shoulder, leg or foot.

  • Weak, switched-off muscles. When a nerve is not sending strong signals, the muscles it supplies become weak and can shrink if they are not gently worked.

  • Stiff joints and tight tissue. When a limb does not move normally, the shoulder, elbow, wrist, ankle and foot can become stiff and painful, making recovery harder.

  • Compensating movements. The body finds other ways to move, such as shrugging the shoulder or dragging the foot. This can become a habit that limits recovery.

  • Reduced feeling and low use. When feeling is reduced, the limb is used less and can be injured without you noticing, so it gets weaker still.

The way forward is guided, gentle rehabilitation: keep the joints moving, wake up weak muscles step by step, retrain movement and protect the limb while the nerve recovers. Recovery depends on how severe the nerve injury is, and this programme is built to give you the best support from home.

The full programme

Your 30-day recovery journey, phase by phase

Three phases, each building on the last. Just 15 to 20 minutes of guided exercises each morning.

  1. 1 Days 1 to 10

    Phase 1: Protect the limb, keep joints moving and wake up the muscles

    Goal: Keep the joints supple, protect the weak limb and start gentle muscle activation.

    • Safe positioning and handling of the weak arm or leg
    • Gentle assisted movements to keep joints supple, done by you or your caregiver with the physiotherapist guiding
    • Gentle muscle activation exercises, as far as the nerve allows
    • Skin care and safety for parts with reduced feeling
    • Caregiver training: how to help safely, without pulling on the limb
  2. 2 Days 11 to 20

    Phase 2: Strengthen and retrain movement

    Goal: Build strength in the muscles that are responding and retrain proper movement.

    • Step-by-step strengthening of the shoulder, elbow, hand, ankle or foot muscles that are responding
    • Movement retraining, so you use the right muscles instead of compensating
    • Sensory re-education exercises to help with reduced feeling
    • Balance and walking practice for a dragging foot
    • Simple activities that encourage use of the affected arm or leg
  3. 3 Days 21 to 30

    Phase 3: Build function and independence

    Goal: Use the arm or leg for daily tasks more confidently and independently.

    • Practise everyday tasks: dressing, reaching, carrying, walking and stairs
    • Task-based training with repeated practice, adapted to your ability
    • A long-term home routine you or your caregiver can keep up
    • Advice on when to go back to your doctor or specialist
    • Maintenance exercises to help keep joints supple and muscles working

Honest answers

Your questions, answered honestly

Can exercises really help Erb's palsy or injection palsy?

How much recovery is possible depends on how badly the nerve was injured, and nobody can promise a particular result. What guided physiotherapy can do is keep joints supple, wake up and strengthen the muscles that are responding, retrain movement and prevent stiffness and other problems while the nerve recovers. That is why you watch the free sample first.

My child has Erb's palsy. Can this programme help?

Yes, with a parent or caregiver taking part. You join the live session, and the physiotherapist shows you safe positioning and handling and simple exercises to do with your child. Please have your child checked by a doctor first, and never pull on your child's arm.

The nerve injury happened a long time ago. Is it too late?

It is not too late to work on stiffness, the strength that is present, movement habits and daily function, although how much changes depends on the injury and how long ago it happened. Our physiotherapist will be honest with you about what is realistic.

I am afraid of doing the wrong exercise and making things worse.

That is a fair worry, and it is why the sessions are live. Phase 1 uses gentle, safe movements that we guide step by step. If an exercise causes sharp pain, or you notice new or sudden weakness, numbness or swelling, stop and message us on WhatsApp, and see a doctor if it does not settle.

Does this replace seeing a doctor or specialist?

No. Some nerve injuries need a specialist review or surgery, and only your doctor can advise on that. This programme works alongside your medical care and does not replace a diagnosis or treatment.

What do I need to join the live sessions?

A smartphone, tablet or computer and a steady internet connection. We send the session link on WhatsApp once your booking is confirmed. Sessions are usually 15 to 20 minutes, and we work with you to find times that fit your day. For a child or someone who needs help, a family member should be there to join in.
